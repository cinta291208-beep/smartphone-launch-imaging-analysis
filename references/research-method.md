# Research Method

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Manufacturer technical specifications and product pages.
2. Manufacturer newsroom/release material for launch claims, dates, availability, and official wording.
3. Original creator full reviews and hands-on videos/posts.
4. Independent specialist reviews and measurements.
5. Reputable media reports summarizing tests or creator findings.
6. Search-result snippets or aggregators only as discovery aids.

Do not use level 5 or 6 to overwrite a directly available level 1–4 source.

## Freshness

For a current launch, record the research date. Search separately for:
- launch announcement;
- launch-day hands-on;
- embargo/full review;
- camera-specific review/sample comparison;
- later firmware/update findings.

A later full review should normally supersede an earlier hands-on summary while the earlier item can remain as historical context.

## Fact ledger

Before writing, maintain a compact ledger:

| Claim | Type | Model | Source | Date | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| variable aperture range | official spec | device | URL | date | high | region |
| flare under side light | reviewer observation | device | URL | date | medium | tested at f/x |

Types: official fact, official claim, reviewer observation, third-party interpretation, synthesis.

## Verification

Cross-check unusually important or surprising claims. Do not confuse:
- equivalent focal length with physical focal length;
- optical zoom with sensor crop marketed as optical-quality;
- ghosting with veiling flare;
- stabilization mode with base OIS;
- launch software behavior with permanent hardware limitations.

## Writing

Prefer concrete conditions over generic praise. “At f/1.48 under dark conditions with a strong lateral light source, reviewer X observed flare” is stronger than “the lens has bad flare.”
