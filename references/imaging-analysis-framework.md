# Imaging Analysis Framework

Analyze the camera as a system rather than a megapixel list.

## 1. Optics
- equivalent focal length and field of view
- aperture, including variable aperture behavior
- lens construction only when documented
- minimum focus/macro
- periscope/folded optics
- optical artifacts: flare, ghosting, softness, breathing

Ask: what new physical control or constraint does the optical system introduce?

## 2. Sensor and capture hardware
- sensor format/size if documented
- resolution and pixel binning
- stabilization
- readout implications when independently tested
- autofocus system

Ask: is the hardware change intended for light gathering, detail, motion, zoom, or workflow?

## 3. Computational photography
- HDR/fusion pipelines
- portrait/depth
- style/color controls
- subject tracking
- low-light processing
- generative/AI editing only when relevant to capture or finishing

Separate capture-time algorithms from post-capture editing.

## 4. Zoom system
Map every marketed zoom point to its source camera/sensor crop. Explain transitions and tradeoffs rather than repeating “x times zoom.”

## 5. Video
Cover resolution/frame rate, Dolby Vision/HDR, ProRes/RAW, Log, genlock/timecode, stabilization, cinematic modes, slow motion, time-lapse, low-light video, external recording, and post-shoot effects where available.

Ask whether the device is improving “video quality” or the entire production workflow.

## 6. Audio
Treat microphones, spatial audio, wind reduction, voice/music mixing, and post-recording audio control as part of the imaging system when the launch emphasizes them.

## 7. Front camera and interaction
Cover autofocus, Center Stage/auto framing, orientation, eye-contact aids, dual capture, external-screen preview, rear-camera selfie, remote preview, and foldable postures.

## 8. Professional control
Look for manual aperture, shutter, ISO, white balance, focus, histogram, zebras, waveform, LUT/log preview, file formats, and synchronization.

## 9. Real-world review questions
- Does the new hardware improve every scene or only specific conditions?
- Is the default automatic mode using the new capability?
- What is the cost in weight, heat, battery, processing delay, or complexity?
- Do reviewers reproduce the manufacturer's claim?
- Does a firmware version affect the result?

## 10. Competitor implications
Compare strategies:
- large sensor vs variable aperture;
- periscope reach vs multi-focal sensor crop;
- computational color vs manual style controls;
- capture quality vs professional video workflow;
- foldable form factor vs conventional camera ergonomics.

Do not declare a technical winner without a narrowly defined, evidence-based criterion.
